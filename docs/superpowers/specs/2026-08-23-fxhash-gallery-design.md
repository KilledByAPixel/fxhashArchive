# fxhash viewer — the gallery

Design, 2026-08-23. Status: awaiting review.

## Why

The archive holds 420 generators that run with no network at all. On the site they
are a grid of square previews with a badge. A grid is a catalog; it is not a place.
This is a museum for exactly those 420 pieces — rooms you walk through, walls the
work hangs on, and, when you step up to a painting, the real piece running from its
real seed, with its whole edition a keypress away.

Only archived projects are in it, because only those can be run from here, which is
what makes a painting more than a picture of one.

## Decisions already made

These came out of the brainstorming and are not open for re-litigation here:

- **Walk freely, click to approach.** First-person WASD and mouse look, and clicking
  any painting glides you square in front of it. That squared-up view is where the
  piece comes alive.
- **Minted editions only.** Stepping a piece walks the real edition using archived
  seeds. No random unminted seeds — every piece shown exists on chain, consistent
  with the rest of the site.
- **The walk is chronological.** The corridor follows the platform's timeline, with
  era markers where each era begins, and every artist with more than one archived
  piece has a room off it. (First built as seven era halls with rooms only for
  artists with five or more pieces; that clustered the rooms at the start and left
  the rest a bare corridor, so it became a loop — see Geometry.)
- **Built at build time.** Layout and atlases are generated by a script into
  `public/data`, the client is thin, and everything is local — the release zip gets
  the gallery for free.

## Not in scope

Minimap, multiplayer, WebXR, sound, non-archived projects, hand-modelled
architecture, per-painting sizes, any ranking by money. A hand-modelled skin over
the generated layout is a plausible later project; nothing here should prevent it.

## Routes and entry points

```
/gallery                       the museum; spawn in the lobby
/gallery?project=<id>          spawn 3 m in front of that painting, facing it
/gallery?room=<room id>        spawn just inside that room's door, facing in
```

The route is a sibling of `Layout` in the route table, not a child: the page is
full-bleed with no site header or footer. A small HUD (section "HUD") carries the
way back. The header gains a **Gallery** tab after Artists. The project page shows
"See it in the gallery" for an archived project, and the artist page shows "This
artist has a room in the gallery" when they do; both are plain links.

`?project` and `?room` are read from the hash route's search params. An unknown
value falls back to the lobby with no error — a stale link is not a broken page.

## Build step

`scripts/build-gallery.mjs` is the entry point; all logic that can be pure lives in
`scripts/gallery-lib.mjs` and is unit-tested. Run with `npm run gallery`. Same rule
as `npm run summary`: rerun after any change to the archived set, or the gallery
goes stale. The README's developer section says so.

### Inputs

- `public/data/generators/manifest.json` — the archived set. Its keys are the only
  projects in the gallery.
- `public/data/tokens/index-*.json` — name, slug, `createdAt`, author, flag.
- `public/data/collaborations.json` — members of collaboration contracts.
- `public/data/thumbs/<id>.<ext>` — the preview images.

Projects with a hidden flag (`MALICIOUS`, `HIDDEN`, `REPORTED`, `AUTO_DETECT_COPY`,
kept in step with `HIDDEN_FLAGS` in `src/lib/data.ts` and `build-summary.mjs`) are
excluded even if a generator is on disk. An archived project with no thumbnail on
disk is still hung, with a flat dark tile; the script warns.

### Room assignment

- An artist key is the author's `tz` address. A collaboration (author is a `KT1`)
  has no single artist: it hangs on the corridor and its caption credits every
  member, via the same `creditLine` that `summary-lib.mjs` uses.
- `SOLO_MIN = 2`. Any artist with more than one non-collaboration archived
  project gets a room holding all of them. With the current archive that is 78
  rooms holding 296 pieces; 124 hang on the corridor — the 120 artists with a
  single archived piece, and the collaborations. (The first build used 5, which
  gave 19 rooms, all but seven of them for artists who started in 2021 — so the
  rooms clustered at the start and the rest of the building was empty corridor.)
- Eras, from `createdAt` (UTC):

  | id        | label              | rule                       |
  |-----------|--------------------|----------------------------|
  | `2021`    | 2021 · Nov–Dec     | anything before 2022       |
  | `2022-q1` | 2022 · Jan–Mar     | quarter                    |
  | `2022-q2` | 2022 · Apr–Jun     | quarter                    |
  | `2022-q3` | 2022 · Jul–Sep     | quarter                    |
  | `2022-q4` | 2022 · Oct–Dec     | quarter                    |
  | `2023-q1` | 2023 · Jan–Mar     | quarter                    |
  | `2023-on` | 2023 · Apr onward  | anything from 2023-04-01   |

  Eras are markers, not rooms: a sign above the first piece of each era along the
  walk, a place the Rooms menu and `?room=<era>` can take you to. Every era has
  a marker even if nothing hangs in it — one with no corridor piece stands at the
  door of its first artist, and failing that where the previous era's marker is.
- Order everywhere is `createdAt` ascending, ties by project id. Rooms go round
  the loop in order of their artist's earliest piece, alternating outside and
  inside; corridor pieces go round in date order. The two sequences are each
  chronological along the walk, not interleaved with each other — a room is
  placed where the next stretch of wall on its side holds it.

### Geometry

Units are metres, y up. Constants, all in `gallery-lib.mjs`:

```
HALL_W         8      corridor width
WALL_H         4      ceiling height everywhere
WALL_T         0.3    wall thickness
WALL_OFFSET    0.17   a painting or sign stands this far off the room rectangle's edge:
                      WALL_T/2 to the wall's inside face, plus 0.02 clear of it
PAINTING       1.2    painting side (all square)
EYE_Y          1.6    painting centre height, and the camera's eye height
SPACING        3      painting pitch along a corridor wall, centre to centre
DOOR_W         2      door width;  DOOR_H 3
OPENING_W      4      the lobby's opening onto the loop
ROOM_MIN       6      the smallest room, for up to ROOM_MIN_PIECES (4) pieces
ROOM_MID       8      where a room with more pieces starts growing from
ROOM_GAP       2      wall left between adjacent rooms on the same side
CORNER         1      no painting centre closer than this to a room corner
INNER_MARGIN   10     inner rooms keep this clear of each leg's ends (see below)
INNER_MAX      8      a room wider than this goes outside the loop
LOOP_MIN       18     shortest leg: two of the widest inner rooms face to face
LOBBY          8      lobby is LOBBY × LOBBY
```

**The loop.** One corridor, `HALL_W` wide, running out along four straight legs
of equal length `L` joined by four 8 × 8 corners, and back into the lobby: the
lobby is the south-west corner, leg A runs north from its opening, then corner
NW, leg B east, corner NE, leg C south, corner SE, and leg D west into the
lobby's open east side. Walking clockwise brings you back where you started.
The corridor is continuous through the corners — no walls between a leg and its
corner. Each leg has an outside wall (away from the courtyard) and an inside
wall.

**Rooms** hang off both walls of every leg. A room's side `s` is `ROOM_MIN`
for up to four pieces and otherwise the smallest `s ≥ ROOM_MID` whose walls hold
them at pitch (see hanging, below) — 31 pieces is 24 m. Rooms are placed in date
order: each takes the next free stretch of wall on its side, from the start of
the current leg onward, moving to the next leg when the current one is full;
outside rooms may use a leg from `ROOM_GAP` in to `ROOM_GAP` short of its end,
inside rooms from `INNER_MARGIN` to `INNER_MARGIN` short, because two legs
meet at every courtyard corner and an inner room at the end of one would sit
where an inner room at the start of the next begins. A room wider than
`INNER_MAX` goes outside regardless. The door is centred on the room's corridor
wall.

**Corridor pieces** fill every free run of corridor wall — legs' walls between the
doors, corners' two outer walls — at pitch `SPACING`, `CORNER` clear of ends
and doors, in walking order: leg A's outside wall then inside wall by distance,
corner NW, and so on round. `L` is the smallest length ≥ `LOOP_MIN`, in steps
of `SPACING`, for which every room finds its stretch and every corridor piece a
slot; found by growing `L` until both hold.

**Hanging in a room.** No blank walls: the room's walls are filled in the order
*facing the door, left, right, door wall left of the door, door wall right of
it*, one piece on each before any wall gets a second (`distribute`), and each
wall's pieces are spread evenly over its usable run, `CORNER` from the corners
and from the door (`spreadOnRun`). Two pieces face you and flank you as you
enter; four use every wall.

**Lobby.** The south-west corner of the loop, 8 × 8. Spawn is its centre, eye
height, facing north into leg A; the title sits above that opening: "fxhash"
and, below it, "420 archived works · 200 artists · 2021–2024", with the numbers
from the data (artists = distinct people credited, collaboration members
included). Its east side is open to leg D.

**Signs.** Four kinds, each a quad on a wall: the lobby title above the lobby's
opening ("fxhash", 3 × 0.5, with the counts line under it, 3 × 0.25); an era sign
above the first corridor piece of each era (3 × 0.5, centred 3.4 m up); room signs
(2.4 × 0.4, above the door on the corridor side and on the wall facing the door
inside); and a plaque under every painting's lower-right corner (0.5 × 0.12:
"Name — Artist, Year"). Their text and placement are in the JSON; they are
rasterised on the client.

**Invariants** (these are the tests):

- every visible archived project is placed exactly once
- no two rooms' floor rectangles overlap (legs, corners, rooms, lobby; era
  markers have zero area)
- every painting lies on an edge of its room's rectangle, offset `WALL_OFFSET`
  inward, facing into the room; no two paintings on one edge are closer than
  `SPACING`, and none is closer than `CORNER` to a corner
- every door or opening joins exactly two rooms, and no wall segment crosses it
- the corridor parts are exactly `leg-a, corner-nw, leg-b, corner-ne, leg-c,
  corner-se, leg-d`, leg D returns into the lobby, opposite legs are equal
- every artist with `SOLO_MIN` or more pieces has a room; rooms stand on both
  sides of the corridor; a room's pieces use two, three or four walls for two,
  three or four-plus pieces
- one era marker per era, zero-area, standing inside a corridor part
- the same inputs produce byte-identical output

### gallery.json

```ts
interface Gallery {
  generatedAt: string
  counts: { paintings: number; artists: number; soloRooms: number; years: [number, number] }
  atlas: { size: number; tile: number; gutter: number; cols: number; files: string[]; small: string[] }
  spawn: Pose
  rooms: Room[]
  walls: Wall[]          // solid segments only; doors are the gaps
  paintings: Painting[]
  signs: Sign[]
}
interface Pose { x: number; z: number; yaw: number }              // eye height is implied
interface Room {
  id: string             // a corridor part, an era id, an artist's tz address, or 'lobby'
  kind: 'lobby' | 'hall' | 'solo' | 'era'   // 'hall' is a leg or corner; 'era' a zero-area marker
  title: string          // era label or artist name
  rect: { x: number; z: number; w: number; d: number }
  entry: Pose            // just inside the door, facing in — the Rooms menu target
}
interface Wall { x1: number; z1: number; x2: number; z2: number; y0: number; y1: number }
interface Painting {
  project: number; slug: string; name: string; artist: string; year: number
  room: string
  x: number; z: number; yaw: number   // centre on the wall, facing direction
  tile: number                        // index into the atlas sequence
}
interface Sign {
  text: string; kind: 'title' | 'era' | 'room' | 'plaque'
  x: number; y: number; z: number; yaw: number; w: number; h: number
}
```

Walls above a door (`y0 = DOOR_H`) and above an opening are segments like any
other; the client's collider ignores any segment with `y0 > 0`. A painting's `tile`
is its index in project-id order; its atlas file is `floor(tile / cols²)`, column
`tile % cols`, row `floor((tile mod cols²) / cols)`. About 40 KB.

### Atlases

`public/data/gallery/atlas-<n>.webp`: 4096², tile 256, gutter 4 — cell 264, 15 × 15
= 225 tiles per file, so two files hold 420 with about 30 slots spare. The gutter is
the tile's own edge pixels extended (`sharp().extend({ extendWith: 'copy' })`) so
mipmaps do not bleed neighbours in. Thumbnails are resized to 256 with
`fit: 'contain'` on black — the grid letterboxes on black, and alpha is flattened
the same way. `atlas-<n>-small.webp`: 2048², tile 128, gutter 2, cell 132, the same
15 × 15 grid and the same tile numbering, for phones. WebP quality 82, matching
`compress-thumbnails.mjs`. Expected 2–4 MB per large file, under 1 MB per small.

The script prints the byte total of everything it wrote. The existing
`public/data/thumbs/` stays — the grid still uses it.

## Client

### Files

```
src/pages/GalleryPage.tsx      route: loading / fallback / mounts the scene; lazy-loaded
src/gallery/types.ts           the Gallery interface above
src/gallery/load.ts            fetch gallery.json + pick and load the atlases
src/gallery/scene.ts           three.js: meshes from the JSON
src/gallery/labels.ts          canvas atlas for the signs
src/gallery/controls.ts        pointer lock, keys, touch, and the walk loop
src/gallery/collide.ts         circle-vs-segment sliding (pure)
src/gallery/approach.ts        viewing pose, glide, projectedRect (pure where possible)
src/gallery/query.ts           ?project / ?room parsing (pure)
src/gallery/Viewer.tsx         the piece overlay: PieceFrame + edition bar
src/gallery/Hud.tsx            crosshair caption, Rooms menu, back link, hints
```

`three` (^0.185) is a runtime dependency imported only under `src/gallery/`.
`GalleryPage` is `React.lazy`, and it imports the scene module dynamically only
after the WebGL probe passes — so three.js is its own chunk and is never fetched
where it cannot run. No `three/examples` — pointer-lock controls are a few dozen
lines, and owning them keeps touch and the viewing handoff in one place.

### Loading

`gallery.json` loads first — it names the atlas files — then both atlas files load
in parallel. The small atlases are chosen
when `renderer.capabilities.maxTextureSize < 4096` or the screen's shorter side is
under 800 px. Textures: `SRGBColorSpace`, mipmaps on, anisotropy at the device
maximum, `LinearMipmapLinearFilter`. The page shows the site's usual loading text
until the scene's first frame.

### Scene

- Walls: one box per `Wall` segment (length × (y1 − y0) × WALL_T), merged into one
  geometry. Floor and ceiling: one plane each per room, merged. `MeshLambertMaterial`,
  dark neutral — walls around `#2a2a2a`, floor and ceiling `#1a1a1a`, the hemisphere
  light telling them apart — so the pictures are the light in the room, as on the
  rest of the site.
- Paintings: one quad per painting with UVs into its atlas tile, merged into one
  mesh per atlas — two draw calls for all 420. `MeshBasicMaterial` (unlit) with the
  atlas as map, `toneMapped: false`: the art must look like the art. A frame: a
  thin dark box 0.06 wider than the painting each side, all frames merged.
- Signs and plaques: one runtime canvas atlas (2048²) drawn at load from the
  `signs` list, then one merged quad mesh. System font, light grey on transparent.
- Lights: a warm `HemisphereLight` (sky `#fff4e6`, ground `#3a3a3a`, 1.6), a
  warm key `DirectionalLight` (`#fff1dc`, 1.2) angled down the spine, and a cool
  fill (`#cfe0ff`, 0.4) from the other side; `FogExp2` in the background colour
  (`#151515`, density 0.018) for depth. Walls `#7a746c`, floors `#3a3634`,
  ceilings `#2b2b2b` — the first build's `#2a2a2a` walls under a near-black
  ground light rendered as black on every monitor.
- Spot pools: the lamplight a museum throws on the wall around each picture. Not
  a light per painting (four hundred lights is the shader melting) but one radial
  falloff texture, computed once, on an additive quad 2.4 × 3 m behind every
  painting — between the wall face and the frame — all merged into one mesh:
  one draw call for every pool in the building. See `src/gallery/pools.ts`.
- Camera: `PerspectiveCamera`, 70° vertical fov, near 0.1, far 200.

Total triangles are a few thousand; no LOD, no culling work beyond three's own.

### Movement

Desktop: while unlocked, a click on the canvas does one thing only — request
pointer lock. While locked, mouse moves yaw and pitch (pitch clamped ±85°) and a
click approaches whatever painting is under the crosshair. W/A/S/D and the arrow
keys move at 3 m/s; Shift makes it 20 m/s — the halls are up to 105 m long. At
that speed one frame can move further than a wall is thick, so `integrate` takes
any move over 0.25 m in pieces and resolves collision after each; a wall can
never fall between two frames. Movement is in the xz plane at `EYE_Y`.
When pointer lock is lost while walking (Esc), a centred hint says "Click to look
around".

Collision: the player is a circle of radius 0.4 in xz. Each frame, against every
wall segment with `y0 === 0`, the nearest point on the segment is found and the
player is pushed out along the normal; two passes so corners resolve. Segments are
few enough (hundreds) that there is no spatial index.

Touch (any `pointerType === 'touch'`): a drag rotates the view; a tap (under
300 ms, under 10 px travel) on the floor sets a walk target reached in a straight
line at walk speed, stopping early if blocked; a tap on a painting approaches it.
No joystick.

### Approaching and viewing

Trigger: click on a painting while pointer-locked (raycast from the screen centre),
tap on one, or Enter/E while the crosshair caption shows one.

1. Release pointer lock; input is ignored until arrival.
2. Compute the viewing pose: on the painting's normal, at distance
   `d = PAINTING / (2 × FILL × tan(fov/2) × min(1, aspect))` with `FILL = 0.75`, at
   `EYE_Y`, yaw facing the painting, pitch 0. At that pose the painting fills 75 %
   of the viewport's shorter side.
3. Glide there over 600 ms with ease-in-out on position and yaw/pitch. With
   `prefers-reduced-motion`, jump.
4. On arrival, `projectedRect(camera, painting, viewport)` projects the four
   corners and takes their bounding box, in CSS pixels. `Viewer` mounts an
   absolutely positioned `PieceFrame` at that rect (`position: fixed`, no border
   radius — it must coincide with the quad). Recomputed on resize.
5. The edition: `loadSummary()` for `runners`, `loadIterationIds(slug, project)`
   for the ids, `loadProjectIteration(project, tokenId)` for the seed and query —
   the same three calls the project page makes, through the same cache. The first
   minted iteration plays first. `archivedSrc(project, seed, query, hasRunner &&
   !raw)` is the frame's `src`.
6. The bar under the frame: "**Name #n** of N · Artist · Year", ‹ and › to step
   (wrapping), **Random** for a random minted iteration, a link to
   `/token/<slug>`, the same "run the artist's file untouched" toggle the project
   page offers, and **Back**. Left/Right arrow keys step too.
7. Leaving: Back, Esc, a click on the canvas outside the frame, or W/A/S/D. The
   frame unmounts first, then pointer lock is re-requested from the user's gesture
   where there was one (a click); after Esc the "Click to look around" hint shows.

While the frame is mounted the painting quad stays underneath it, so a piece that
takes a second to boot shows its preview until then. A token that was never signed
(no seed) shows the same one-line explanation the project page shows, in place of
the frame. Because every gallery project is archived, there is no streamed path.

Keyboard focus goes to the frame's sandbox when the visitor clicks into the piece,
and then our key handlers do not fire — that is why Back is a visible button and
not only a key.

### HUD

A thin layer over the canvas, `pointer-events: none` except its controls:

- top left: "← fxhash viewer" (to `/`), and the current room's title
- top right: **Rooms**, a button opening a list — the seven era markers, then the
  artists alphabetically (78 of them) — each teleporting to `room.entry` and
  closing the list; corridor legs and corners are walked, not listed
- centre: a 6 px crosshair while pointer-locked; under it, the caption of the
  painting it is over, if any, within 6 m
- bottom: a one-line hint that fades after first use: "WASD to walk · click a
  painting to see it run"

Teleport from the menu is instant, not a glide — the two are different acts.

### Deep links

`?project=<id>`: spawn on the painting's normal 3 m from the wall, facing it, in
walking mode; the piece does not auto-play, since pointer lock and the frame both
want a gesture. `?room=<id>`: spawn at `room.entry`.

## Error handling

- No WebGL (`canvas.getContext('webgl2')` and `webgl` both null): the page shows a
  paragraph saying so and a link to the archived grid at `/artwork`. No three.js
  is loaded for this case — the check runs before the lazy import.
- `gallery.json` fails to load: the site's `LoadError` with retry.
- An atlas fails: its paintings render in flat `#222`; captions, approaching and
  playing still work, since none of those need the image. A console warning names
  the file.
- A piece that does not run is the sandbox's business, exactly as elsewhere — the
  "untouched" toggle is the visitor's diagnostic, as on the project page.
- The page must not leak: on unmount, dispose geometries, materials, textures and
  the renderer, and remove every listener. Navigating away and back twice must not
  double resources.

## Testing

`scripts/gallery-lib.test.mjs` (vitest, pure functions over small fixtures):
- era bucketing, including the "before 2022" and "2023-04-01 onward" edges
- solo threshold; a collaboration never gets or joins a solo room; credits name
  every member
- solo rooms attach to the era of the artist's earliest piece, alternating sides
- the five invariants under "Geometry", on a fixture of ~40 projects and on the
  real data when it is present (skipped otherwise, so CI without the archive passes)
- determinism: two runs are deep-equal

`scripts/build-gallery.mjs` atlas packing: tile → (file, column, row) → pixel rect
for both sizes, and that 420 tiles need exactly two files.

`src/gallery/*.test.ts`: `collide` (slides along a wall, resolves a corner, ignores
`y0 > 0`), `approach` (`viewingPose` distance for landscape and portrait,
`projectedRect` is square and centred at the viewing pose), `query` (valid, missing,
garbage).

`src/pages/GalleryPage.test.tsx`: shows the loading state; shows the no-WebGL
message when the context probe returns null; never imports three in jsdom.

Manual, before calling it done: a desktop walk-through including the pointer-lock ⇄
frame handoff both ways and stepping an edition with the keys; the same on a phone;
`?project` and `?room` links; and the release zip served offline.

## Budget and performance

- Site size: about 6–9 MB against the 66 MiB of GitHub Pages headroom — the script
  reports the real figure and it goes in the commit message.
- JS: three.js is its own chunk, ~150 KB gzipped, loaded only on `/gallery`.
- Runtime: under ten draw calls, a few thousand triangles; target 60 fps on
  integrated graphics, scene built within 200 ms of the data arriving.
- GPU memory: the two large atlases are ~170 MB with mipmaps; the small pair is
  ~43 MB, which is why phones get them.
